Chrome Autofill Can Now Pull Details From Your Gmail: What to Know

A new Chrome feature looks through your email to fill forms. Here is what it does, what Google has and has not explained, and how to switch it off.

Rapid+ Team · 18 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

On this page

Written on 18 November 2026. This is based on Google's announcement of 28 October 2026 as reported by 9to5Google and Decrypt. We did not test the feature, which is limited to the United States, so we describe only what was announced and what reporters pointed out was left unanswered.

Chrome can now fill web forms with details that Gemini finds in your Gmail, such as order numbers, tracking information and flight bookings. Google began rolling the feature out on 28 October 2026. It is optional and limited to people who have turned on Gemini's "Personal Intelligence" in Chrome, but it is a real change: the browser's autofill is no longer limited to what you saved in it.

This article explains what the feature does, what Google says about privacy, which questions reporters say remain open, and how to keep control of it.

The short version

  • "Enhanced autofill" uses Gemini to search your Gmail and suggest information when a web form asks for it. Examples named by Google are order numbers, tracking information and flight bookings.
  • Suggestions appear in the autofill menu under "Suggested by Gemini".
  • It is available only in the United States, starting 28 October, for people who use Gemini in Chrome with Personal Intelligence turned on. It works on desktop, Android and iOS.
  • Google says "sensitive data is encrypted and you remain in control". You can remove individual suggestions or turn the enhanced autofill off in Chrome settings.
  • Reporters note that Google's post does not explain how encryption applies to the Gmail content Chrome reads or what exactly it scans.

What the feature does

Classic autofill stores a name, an address, a phone number and a payment card in the browser, then offers them when a form asks. The new feature goes further. When you reach a form that asks for something you may have received by email, such as a booking reference on an airline's check-in page, Chrome can ask Gemini to look through your Gmail and offer a match.

Google presented it as a convenience for the small, annoying lookups: finding an order number to open a return, or a flight confirmation to start check-in. 9to5Google says the feature can "automatically suggest information pulled directly from Gmail as you fill out online forms". Decrypt points out that the announcement does not say the feature pulls credit card numbers or addresses from Gmail. Standard autofill still handles names and addresses saved in the browser. One outlet reported a longer list of data types, which conflicts with the announcement, so we treat Google's own list of three as the authoritative one.

Who can use it

  • People in the United States.
  • Users of Gemini in Chrome who have Personal Intelligence turned on, which means they chose to connect Gemini to their apps.
  • Desktop, Android and iOS.

Decrypt says Personal Intelligence is not available in Europe for now, so there is no date for a European launch. A separate outlet reported that Google's help page requires users to be 18 or older. Decrypt gave no age limit, so we cannot confirm it from the sources we read.

What Google says about privacy

Google makes two claims. The first is that "sensitive data is encrypted and you remain in control". The second, quoted by Decrypt from a statement in March, is that "Gemini and AI Mode don't train directly on your Gmail inbox or Google Photos library". Decrypt adds that Google does train on limited information, such as specific prompts and the model's responses.

These statements answer some worries and leave others. The questions that remain open, according to Decrypt, are:

  • How encryption applies to the Gmail content that Chrome reads, and what exactly it scans.
  • Whether credit card numbers or addresses could come from Gmail in the future, since the announcement does not rule it out.
  • When the feature will reach other countries.

Encryption in transit and in storage protects data from outsiders. It does not tell you what the service itself does with the data it can read, which is the part people usually mean when they ask about privacy.

How to keep control

  1. Decide whether to connect Personal Intelligence at all. The feature needs it. Decrypt says users choose whether to connect their apps and can disconnect at any time.
  2. Review Chrome's autofill settings. Google says you can turn enhanced autofill off in Chrome settings. Neither 9to5Google nor Decrypt gave the exact menu path, so look under the autofill section of Settings.
  3. Remove suggestions you do not want. Individual suggestions can be removed from the menu.
  4. Use a separate profile for sensitive mail. If you keep a work or private account in a different browser profile, Gemini in the first profile has nothing to read there. Our article on browser profiles explains how the separation works.

None of this is a reason to panic. It is a reason to notice that a browser with an AI assistant becomes a path between the web and your private data, which is the same pattern that researchers worry about in our article on AI browsers and prompt injection.

Frequently asked questions

Does Chrome read my Gmail by default?

No. The feature is limited to people who use Gemini in Chrome with Personal Intelligence turned on, in the United States. If you have not connected your apps to Gemini, it does not apply.

What data does it fill in?

Google's announcement names order numbers, tracking information and flight bookings. Standard autofill still handles names and addresses saved in the browser.

Is it available outside the United States?

Not at launch. Google has not given a date for other countries. Personal Intelligence is reported to be unavailable in Europe for now.

Does Google train its models on my emails?

Google says Gemini and AI Mode do not train directly on your Gmail inbox. It also says it trains on limited information such as prompts and the model's responses, and it has not said how this applies to the autofill feature in particular.

How do I turn it off?

Google says you can disable enhanced autofill in Chrome settings, remove single suggestions and disconnect Personal Intelligence at any time. Look in the autofill section of Chrome's settings.
