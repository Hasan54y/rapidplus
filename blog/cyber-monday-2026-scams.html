Scams & Safety

Cyber Monday scams in large 3D letters beside a phone showing a notification saying Your parcel is on hold, with the FedEx, UPS, Visa logos

Attorneys general, police and Amazon all issued warnings this week. Here they are in one place, with a nine-step checklist before you pay.

Rapid+ Team · 28 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

On this page

Written on 28 November 2026. The warnings come from the North Carolina and Vermont attorneys general, a University of Central Florida computer scientist, the Fresno County Sheriff's Office and Amazon, as reported by local news on 27 and 28 November, and from a threat report by the security firm Bfore. The domain counts are Bfore's own research.

Cyber Monday is on 30 November, and the National Retail Federation estimates about 66 million Americans plan to shop that day. Scammers know it too. Fake shops built in minutes, delivery texts that are not from the courier and phone calls with a convincing voice are all doing the rounds. This guide gathers this week's warnings into one checklist you can use before you pay.

The short version

  • Type the shop's address yourself. Do not shop from ads, social media posts or links in messages.
  • Fake stores use stolen photos, fake reviews and countdown timers, and often want hard-to-trace payment such as gift cards, wire transfers or crypto.
  • Delivery problem texts are rarely genuine. Check tracking in the carrier's or shop's own app.
  • If someone calls about your order or your bill, hang up and contact the company directly.
  • Pay with a credit card, keep receipts, and call your bank at once if something goes wrong.

Fake shops that look real

North Carolina Attorney General Jeff Jackson's office warns that fake online stores use "stolen photos, fake reviews and countdowns" and often take payments that are hard to trace. People who pay "will often either receive a fake product or nothing at all."

Making these stores has become easy. Kevin Moran, a computer science professor at the University of Central Florida, told WESH that "AI tools have made it incredibly easy for people to create very close look-alike stores", which are then promoted on social media. His advice is simple: type the real store address into your browser instead of following a link.

The security firm Bfore reviewed 2,539 Black Friday and Cyber Monday themed web addresses and flagged 306 as threats, with 253 rated a direct risk to shoppers. It found 654 of those addresses were registered on or after 1 July 2026, with registrations peaking in October. Bfore also tracks a fake-store kit behind 2,707 stores, adding about 600 new addresses a week. Its advice to the public: "Don't click Black Friday ads or social links to shop."

Delivery texts and fake account alerts

A message saying a parcel cannot be delivered, or that your account needs checking, is the most common lure of the season. Moran says fake delivery notices from FedEx, UPS or online stores "are rarely legitimate and should be ignored and reported." The North Carolina attorney general's office adds that carriers will not ask for details that way, so check tracking on the carrier's official site or app.

Amazon spokesperson Scott Seroka told ABC30 that scammers send "fake delivery notification messages, fake account alert messages." Amazon says customers can paste a suspicious message into its Alexa for Shopping assistant to check whether it really came from Amazon, and its app has real-time tracking for genuine orders.

Phone calls that sound right

Moran warns that impersonation calls may use an AI-generated voice to ask for card details or to "complete" an order. In Vermont, Attorney General Charity Clark's office warned of a utility disconnection scam, where a caller threatens to cut your power unless you pay immediately. In every case the answer is the same: hang up, then contact the company using a number you find yourself.

How to pay safely

Every official this week gave the same payment advice. Use a credit card, which lets you dispute a charge. Avoid gift cards, wire transfers and crypto. "There's no consumer protection in cryptocurrency," Clark said. "Once it's gone, it's completely gone." Bfore adds: do not pay for products or hotel bookings over WhatsApp or bank transfer, and never pay "shipping fees" for a prize or giveaway.

Two smaller habits help too. The North Carolina attorney general's office advises not entering card details on public Wi-Fi, and buying gift cards from behind the counter rather than from a rack, because scammers tamper with cards on display.

Your Cyber Monday checklist

  1. Type the shop's address, or use its official app.
  2. Search the shop's name with the word "scam" and read reviews from other buyers, as the Fresno County Sheriff's Office suggests.
  3. Be wary of 50 to 80 percent discounts, countdown timers and "only 3 left" warnings. Bfore says they are designed to rush you.
  4. Check for a real address and contact details, and watch for poor grammar.
  5. Pay by credit card. Never by gift card, wire transfer or crypto.
  6. Ignore delivery links in texts and track orders in the shop's app.
  7. Hang up on unexpected calls about orders or bills.
  8. Keep receipts and confirmation emails.
  9. Shop in a separate browser profile so a rushed click does not touch your email and bank.

If you think you have been scammed

Call your bank or card issuer straight away, using the number on your card, and ask them to block the card and dispute the charge. Bfore gives the same advice: "contact your bank or card issuer immediately". Change the password for any account you typed into the fake site. Then report it: North Carolina residents can file at ncdoj.gov or call 1-877-5-NO-SCAM, Vermont residents can contact the state's Consumer Assistance Program, and anyone in the US can report fraud to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

Frequently asked questions

When is Cyber Monday 2026?

Monday 30 November 2026, the Monday after Thanksgiving.

What is the most common Cyber Monday scam?

Fake online stores and fake delivery texts top every warning this week. Both rely on you clicking a link instead of going to the real site.

Is it safe to buy from an ad on social media?

Be careful. Fake stores are promoted through social media ads. Find the shop yourself by typing its address.

How do I check if an Amazon message is real?

Amazon says you can paste the message into Alexa for Shopping to check it, or look in your Amazon app's orders and messages.

What is the safest way to pay online?

A credit card, because you can dispute fraudulent charges. Avoid gift cards, wire transfers and crypto.
