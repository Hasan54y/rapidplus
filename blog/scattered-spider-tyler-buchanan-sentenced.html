Scattered Spider Suspected Ringleader Tyler Buchanan Gets Six Years: What the Case Shows

The last of five defendants is sentenced. The numbers, the other four sentences and how phishing like this works on ordinary people.

Rapid+ Team · 24 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

On this page

Written on 24 November 2026. This relies on The Record's report of 18 November. Two BBC pages and the US Justice Department's press release could not be read, so we do not repeat claims from them, and we do not cover the other Scattered Spider case that BBC reporting links, because we could not verify it. The restitution hearing date comes from The Record, and the year is not stated there.

A British man called Tyler Buchanan has been sentenced to six years in a US federal prison for his part in a phishing campaign that stole more than $11.6 million in cryptocurrency. The 24-year-old from Dundee was described as the suspected ringleader of Scattered Spider, the loose group of young hackers linked to the attack on casino giant MGM Resorts. He is the last of five defendants to be sentenced, which makes this the closing chapter of one of the best-known cybercrime cases of the decade.

The short version

  • Tyler Robert Buchanan, 24, of Dundee, Scotland, got six years in federal prison, in a case brought in the Central District of California.
  • He was arrested at Palma Airport in Spain in June 2024, and was named in November 2024 with four others.
  • Prosecutors say he admitted to a phishing campaign to steal millions of dollars' worth of virtual currency. The amount stolen from individuals in the US was more than $11.6 million.
  • The MGM Resorts attack is described as costing an estimated $100 million, a figure The Record takes from earlier reporting, not from the court.
  • A restitution hearing, which decides what he must repay, is set for 11 December.

The sentence

The Record says Buchanan was sentenced on a Tuesday. The article is dated 18 November, so the date was probably 17 November, though the report does not state it. The US Attorney's Office for the Central District of California said he admitted taking part in a phishing campaign "to steal millions of dollars' worth of virtual currency". Prosecutors told the court in a sentencing memo that the crimes had "severe, life-altering consequences for many victims".

The five were charged together in November 2024, according to The Record, and Buchanan is the last to be sentenced. The article lists the targets in the case as including Coinbase, Twilio, Mailchimp, LastPass and the MGM Resorts ransomware attack.

What Scattered Spider is

Scattered Spider is the name researchers gave to a loose network of mostly young, English-speaking attackers. Microsoft called it "one of the most dangerous financial criminal groups", as quoted by The Record. It is known less for advanced code than for talking its way in: pretending to be employees or IT staff, tricking help desks and sending convincing phishing messages. The Record's article does not go into the group's methods beyond the phishing campaign.

What the case teaches ordinary users

You do not need to run a casino to learn from this. The people hurt directly here were individuals who lost cryptocurrency to phishing. The same tools work on anyone:

  • Phishing messages copy real brands. A fake login page can look identical to the real one.
  • Passwords can be handed over without noticing. You type them into a page you trust.
  • Passkeys resist this. A passkey will not work on a lookalike site, which is why we cover them in our passkey guide.
  • Crypto is hard to get back. There is usually no bank to reverse a transfer.

How to protect yourself from phishing

  1. Go to the site yourself. Type the address or use a bookmark, and do not follow a link in a message.
  2. Check the full address before you log in. Our guide to spotting fake pages lists what to look for.
  3. Use two-step verification with an app or security key, instead of a text message where possible.
  4. Treat urgency as a warning. "Your account will be closed" is the oldest trick.
  5. Keep crypto on a device you control for large amounts, and double-check every address you send to. The Optimizely attack showed that even a correct page can be tampered with.
  6. Use a separate browser profile for finance. See our profiles guide.

What happens next

A restitution hearing is scheduled for 11 December, where the court will decide what Buchanan must repay. Ordering repayment and actually collecting it are different things, so victims may recover little. Our report on the current Transport for London incident mentions the 2024 breach there that was linked to the group. The new incident is separate and no group has been named for it.

Frequently asked questions

How long was Tyler Buchanan sentenced for?

Six years in federal prison, according to The Record. He is 24 and from Dundee, Scotland.

What did he do?

He admitted taking part in a phishing campaign to steal millions of dollars in virtual currency. The amount taken from individuals in the US was more than $11.6 million.

Was he behind the MGM attack?

The Record lists the MGM Resorts ransomware attack among the targets in the case. MGM's cost is described as an estimated $100 million, from earlier reporting. The article does not give details of his role in that attack.

Who else was sentenced?

Four others: Noah Urban got 10 years, Ahmed Elbadawy and Evans Osiebo got 45 months each, and Joel Evans got two years. Buchanan was the last of the five.

What is a restitution hearing?

It is a hearing where the court decides how much a convicted person must repay to victims. Buchanan's is set for 11 December.
