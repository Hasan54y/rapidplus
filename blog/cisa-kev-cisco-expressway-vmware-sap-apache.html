CISA Adds Four Exploited Flaws: Cisco Expressway, VMware vCenter, Apache Roller and SAP NetWeaver

A hardcoded password in Cisco Expressway, a spy group's VMware zero-day and two more. The four flaws attackers are using right now.

Rapid+ Team · 24 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

On this page

Written on 24 November 2026. This is based on SecurityWeek's report of 23 November on four flaws that the US cyber agency CISA added to its list of known exploited vulnerabilities on 20 November. We could not read two other reports because they blocked our reader, and we did not read Cisco's advisory, so affected version ranges are not listed here. Check the vendor pages for those.

On 20 November 2026 CISA added four flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list, which means attackers are already using them. The most serious is CVE-2026-76466 in Cisco Expressway, a hardcoded password weakness rated 9.8 out of 10 that can give an attacker full control. The others affect VMware vCenter, Apache Roller and SAP NetWeaver. If you run any of these products, today is a patching day. If you do not, there is still a useful lesson about how quickly attackers move.

The short version

  • Cisco Expressway, CVE-2026-76466 (9.8): a hardcoded credentials flaw that lets attackers run commands as root. Cisco patched it on 21 October in release X15.5.20.
  • VMware vCenter, CVE-2026-123890 (critical): exploited as a zero-day by a China-linked espionage group called Armory Castle, also tracked as Warp Panda.
  • Apache Roller, CVE-2026-129448 (critical): patched on 17 November and exploited as a zero-day.
  • SAP NetWeaver, CVE-2025-42884 (6.5): a medium flaw fixed in November 2025, now confirmed as exploited.
  • CISA told US federal agencies to patch the Cisco flaw within three days.

What the "known exploited" list means

CISA keeps a public catalogue of software flaws that have been used in real attacks. Being on it is a stronger signal than a high score, because most flaws with a high score are never exploited. For US federal agencies it comes with a deadline. For everyone else it is a plain message: this one is being used, patch it first.

A flaw can reach the list in two ways. Either it was a zero-day, used before a fix existed, or it was an old flaw that attackers found still unpatched in the wild. This batch contains both kinds.

Cisco Expressway: a password baked into the software

Expressway is Cisco's gateway for video and voice traffic. It comes in two forms: Expressway-C, the core, and Expressway-E, the edge that faces the internet. SecurityWeek says the flaw affects devices running vulnerable versions and set up in a cluster. A "hardcoded credentials" bug means the software contains a fixed password that cannot be changed and that anyone who finds it can use. Here, an attacker can use it to run system commands with root privileges, the highest level on the machine.

The Russian security firm F6 warned that attackers began exploiting the flaw shortly after proof-of-concept code was published, and that warning came two days before CISA's alert. SecurityWeek links a related issue in which persistent cookies and weak certificate checks could let an attacker reach the database service and call internal commands. The article does not say how many devices were breached or how far attackers got. Cisco's advisory is named cisco-sa-vcs-static-cred-hr7EWZ8t.

What to do

  1. Check whether you run Expressway, and which versions. The fix is in release X15.5.20.
  2. Update clustered devices first, as they are the exposed configuration.
  3. Look for signs of a break-in. A patch closes the door, but it does not remove an attacker who is already inside.
  4. Limit who can reach the management interface, and keep it off the open internet.

VMware vCenter: a spy group's zero-day

vCenter is the control panel for many companies' virtual servers, which makes it a favourite target. CVE-2026-123890 is rated critical and was exploited as a zero-day by Armory Castle, a China-linked espionage group that is also tracked as Warp Panda. SecurityWeek's article does not say what the flaw allows or which versions are affected. Treat any vCenter that can be reached from outside as urgent, and follow VMware's advisory for the fix.

Apache Roller: patched and attacked within days

Apache Roller is open-source blogging software. CVE-2026-129448 is rated critical and was patched on 17 November. It was exploited as a zero-day, and CISA listed it three days later. No attacker has been named, and the article does not describe what the flaw does. If you run Roller, update to the patched version now. If you do not know whether you run it, check, because blogging tools are often forgotten servers.

SAP NetWeaver: an old fix that people skipped

CVE-2025-42884 is rated 6.5. It involves access to an unintended JNDI provider and could let an unauthenticated attacker read or change server information. SAP fixed it in its November 2025 updates, and SecurityWeek says there were no reports of exploitation before CISA's alert. A year later it is being used. This is the pattern behind the rise in ransomware that Beazley describes: attackers keep going after older flaws on systems that were never updated.

What to take from it

  • Patch by exploitation, not by score. A 6.5 that attackers use beats a 9.8 that nobody does.
  • Internet-facing gateways come first. Video gateways, virtualisation consoles and web apps are reachable by anyone.
  • Patching is not cleaning. If a flaw was open for weeks, check logs for earlier access.
  • Keep an inventory. You cannot patch what you do not know you run.

For readers who are not administrators, the same idea applies to your own devices. Our report on the Chrome zero-day covers the habit of updating when a vendor says a flaw is being used.

Frequently asked questions

What is CVE-2026-76466?

It is a critical flaw in Cisco Expressway, rated 9.8, caused by hardcoded credentials. An attacker can use it to run commands with root privileges.

Has Cisco fixed it?

SecurityWeek reports that the flaw was patched on 21 October in Expressway Series release X15.5.20. Check Cisco's advisory for the versions that apply to you.

What does the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list mean for me?

It means attackers are already using a flaw. US federal agencies must patch by a deadline, and other organisations should treat it as a top priority.

Who is exploiting the VMware flaw?

SecurityWeek says a China-linked espionage group called Armory Castle, also known as Warp Panda, used it as a zero-day.

I am a home user. Does this affect me?

These are business and server products, so probably not directly. The lesson still applies: install updates on your own devices soon after they appear.
