Vendor Breaches: Why Infoblox, TSMC, Maersk and Irish Credit Unions Were Hit Through Suppliers

Many victims, one weak link. The mid-November breaches that started at a vendor, what is confirmed, and what customers and companies can do.

Rapid+ Team · 23 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

Written on 23 November 2026. Most details come from TechCrunch, Check Point Research's threat report of 16 November and Privacy Guides' weekly roundup, which in turn summarise BleepingComputer and The Record. Several figures here are claims by attackers or estimates, and we mark them. Companies have not confirmed every number.

In one week in mid-November 2026, a security vendor, the world's biggest chipmaker, a shipping giant and dozens of Irish credit unions all reported data theft. In almost every case the break-in did not start at the company named in the headline. It started at a supplier: a migration contractor, a cloud provider, a legacy system or an IT service firm. This article walks through the cases and what they mean for you as a customer.

The short version

  • Infoblox says hackers reached "certain company information" through a compromised third-party data migration vendor.
  • TSMC confirmed a breach at an unnamed cloud provider that exposed employee data. An attacker claims 46,000 records.
  • Maersk confirmed data theft from a legacy system it inherited through an acquisition. An extortion group claims about 100 GB and wants $5 million.
  • Wellington IT, a supplier to about 50 credit unions, was hit, and at least six credit unions have disclosed the impact.
  • ConnectWise ScreenConnect was abused to install remote access tools on at least 288 computers across 48 organisations.

Infoblox: a migration contractor

Infoblox, a company that sells networking and security products, confirmed on 16 November that it was investigating "unauthorized access to certain company information through a compromised third-party data migration vendor". TechCrunch reports that two people with knowledge of the incident said the vendor handles SharePoint data migrations, and that one of them said sensitive customer data was taken. Infoblox did not name the vendor, would not say how many customers were affected, and said it had no sign that its own products, cloud services, software updates or build processes were involved. One person said Infoblox ended its work with the vendor, which the company did not comment on.

TSMC: a cloud provider

TSMC, the Taiwanese chipmaker, confirmed that employee information leaked after a breach at an unnamed cloud provider. Check Point Research reports that a hacker advertised 6.9 GB of allegedly stolen employee data on a criminal forum. Privacy Guides reports that the attacker claims 46,000 employee records, including national ID numbers, addresses, contact details and banking information. Those numbers are the attacker's claims, not TSMC's. TSMC says its own systems and customer data were not affected.

Maersk: a system nobody retired

Maersk, the Danish shipping and logistics company, confirmed unauthorised access to a legacy system it acquired through a purchase. An extortion group called WhiteArmor claims about 100 GB was stolen and demanded $5 million. Maersk says its other systems and customer operations were not affected. This is not a supplier in the usual sense, but it is the same weakness: a system outside the main security effort that holds real data.

Wellington IT: one supplier, many credit unions

Wellington IT is a Belfast-based supplier whose services are used by about 50 credit unions, including 43 in Ireland, according to Privacy Guides' summary of BleepingComputer. The firm said it lacked activity logs showing which member data was compromised, so it could not verify the impact on individual payees. At least six credit unions have disclosed their own impact. The records could include a payee's name, nickname, IBAN, BIC, bank account number, sort code, address and payment reference, and could show which member saved a given payee.

ConnectWise ScreenConnect: a tool for managing other people's computers

Check Point reports that attackers abused ConnectWise's ScreenConnect remote access platform, compromised managed service provider environments and deployed malicious remote access tools on at least 288 computers across 48 organisations. Managed service providers look after IT for many small companies, so one break-in leads to many.

Why the same pattern keeps appearing

  • One supplier holds many customers' data. Breaking in once is cheaper than breaking in 50 times.
  • Suppliers have access, not attention. They hold keys and storage but are not watched like the main company.
  • Old systems stay on. Acquired or retired systems often lose updates and monitoring while keeping data.
  • Customers cannot see any of it. You gave your data to one company and it reached five.

The same thing happened in the Fireworks AI breach that reached Uber and Cursor and in the Optimizely attack on the BBC and CNN.

What you can do as a customer

  1. Share less. Give a company only the data it needs. Skip optional fields like date of birth.
  2. Use unique passwords and answers, so a leak at one place cannot open another.
  3. Expect phishing after a notice. When a company says data was exposed, the next message you get about it may be fake. Go to the company's site yourself.
  4. Read breach letters. They often include free monitoring, and they say which data was taken.
  5. Consider a credit freeze if national ID numbers or bank details were involved, where this is available.

What businesses can ask their suppliers

  • What data do you hold for us, and where?
  • Who at your company and at your own suppliers can reach it?
  • Do you keep activity logs? Wellington IT said it lacked them, and without logs a company cannot say who was hurt.
  • How will you tell us if you are breached, and how quickly?
  • What happens to our data when the contract ends?

Frequently asked questions

Which companies were breached through suppliers this week?

Infoblox (a data migration vendor), TSMC (a cloud provider) and Wellington IT's credit union customers all involved a supplier. Maersk's breach involved a legacy system from an acquisition.

Is the TSMC number confirmed?

No. TSMC confirmed that employee data leaked. The 46,000 records and 6.9 GB figures are the attacker's claims.

What is a supply-chain breach?

A breach where attackers reach a company through one of its suppliers or tools, instead of attacking the company directly.

How can I tell if my data was in a vendor breach?

Companies must usually tell affected people. Watch for a letter or email from the company you dealt with, and check its website directly instead of using links in messages.

Do I need to do anything if I use an Irish credit union?

Check whether your credit union has issued a notice. At least six have disclosed impact. Watch for messages that mention your payees, since scammers use that kind of detail.
