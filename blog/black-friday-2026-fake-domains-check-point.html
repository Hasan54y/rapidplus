Black Friday 2026: 4,000 Fake Shopping Domains, 10,000 Fake Stores and How to Avoid Them

Fake shops, fake refund pages and fake parcel alerts are everywhere before 27 November. The numbers, the tricks and what to check before you pay.

Rapid+ Team · 24 Nov 2026 · 6 min read

On this page

Written on 24 November 2026. The 2026 figures come from Check Point Research as reported by the Swiss site Netzwoche (17 November) and the German site IT-Daily (18 November), and from NordVPN research reported by Money (6 November). We read translations of the German articles and did not find Check Point's own 2026 post. Some well-known "one in eleven" numbers are from 2025, and we keep them apart.

Black Friday is on 27 November 2026, and the fake shops are ready. Check Point Research counted more than 4,000 newly registered web domains that combine sale-event words such as "Black Friday" or "Cyber Monday" with big brand or parcel-company names. Almost one in five was flagged as risky. Separately, NordVPN found more than 10,000 fake fashion stores copying 166 brands. This article gives you the numbers, the tricks and a short checklist you can use at the checkout.

The short version

  • Check Point Research found more than 4,000 new domains between October and 8 November 2026 with Black Friday, Cyber Monday or Singles' Day words, often plus names such as Amazon, AliExpress, Walmart, DHL or FedEx.
  • Almost one in five of them was classified as risky by Check Point's ThreatCloud AI.
  • It counted 647 new domains that add two to four letters after "Fedex", registered from September to 8 November. Not all are necessarily fraudulent.
  • NordVPN reported more than 10,000 fraudulent stores impersonating 166 fashion and luxury brands in 26 countries. 1,549 were fully working at the time.
  • NordVPN says Perplexity recommended two fake stores, and ChatGPT and Gemini cited other sites tied to the network.
  • Safest habit: type the shop's address yourself or use its app, and do not rely on search results, social posts or an AI chatbot.

What Check Point found

The company watches newly registered domains. Its 2026 figures, as reported by IT-Daily and Netzwoche, are:

  • More than 4,000 new domains containing Black Friday, Cyber Monday or Singles' Day terms, alongside brand and parcel-service names, from October to 8 November 2026.
  • Almost one in five of those was classified as risky.
  • 647 new .com domains that add two to four letters after "Fedex", from September to 8 November: 139 in September, 439 in October and 69 in the first eight days of November.
  • An average of 2,458 cyber attacks a week per company in consumer goods and services in September and October 2026, up 28 percent since the start of the year, against 22 percent across all sectors.

Netzwoche cautions that not every lookalike domain is fraudulent. Some belong to genuine resellers or to people who simply bought a name.

Examples Check Point cited

  • A fake Amazon refund page that asks for phone numbers and email addresses.
  • Fake Best Buy and Walmart shops, and imitations of Oakley and AliExpress offers.
  • A fake Amazon giveaway hosted on a Microsoft Azure subdomain that collected personal data through a form.
  • Fake DHL and USPS pages that claim an address must be corrected or a parcel cannot be delivered, and some ask for the card number, expiry date and security code.

What NordVPN found

Money reports NordVPN research on a large network of fake fashion shops. More than 10,000 fraudulent online stores copy 166 brands, including names such as Adidas, Ralph Lauren, Dr. Martens, Crocs and Birkenstock, across 26 countries. At the time of writing, 1,549 were fully operational, and thousands more might launch before Black Friday. France and Germany were the most targeted countries, and NordVPN found 91 domains aimed at the US.

Three details deserve attention:

  • They are built to be found by search. Money says the stores are tuned to appear in Google results, so you do not have to click an advert to land on one.
  • AI assistants can send you there. According to NordVPN, Perplexity recommended two fraudulent stores next to real retailers, and ChatGPT and Gemini cited other sites connected to the network. These are NordVPN's findings, not independent tests.
  • The payment logos lie. The fake shops show PayPal and Klarna logos but do not accept them. They simply collect card details.

Why these scams work

A fake shop needs only a good deal, a believable address and a checkout. Most people judge a site by its look, and a copied design costs nothing. The name that Black Friday gives to a domain makes it look current. The urgency also helps: a countdown timer and "only 3 left" push you to pay before you think.

The padlock in the address bar does not help either. It shows the connection is encrypted, not that the seller is honest, as the FTC says. Our guide to spotting fake shops covers that point in detail.

A 60 second checklist before you pay

  1. Type or bookmark the address. Go to the shop you know, instead of following a link from search, social media or a chatbot. NordVPN gives the same advice.
  2. Read the whole domain. Watch for extra words, hyphens, doubled letters or odd endings such as .top or .cc. The brand name should be right before the first slash and the dot that follows it.
  3. Compare the price with the brand's own site. A big gap on a famous product is a warning sign.
  4. Check the payment options. If the page shows PayPal but the checkout only asks for a card, leave.
  5. Pay by credit card where you can, since it gives the best chance of a refund, or use your phone's wallet.
  6. Be careful with parcel messages. A text saying your parcel is stuck should send you to the carrier's official site or app, not to the link in the message.
  7. Use a separate browser profile for shopping so that a rushed click does not touch your email and banking. See our profiles guide.

If you already entered card details

Call your card issuer straight away, using the number on the card, and say you used a site that may be fake. Ask them to block and replace the card. Watch statements for several weeks. Change the password for any account whose details you typed in. A card that is saved in a shop account is a risk too, as in the Revolve breach.

Frequently asked questions

How many fake Black Friday sites are there?

Check Point counted more than 4,000 new sale-themed domains between October and 8 November 2026, with almost one in five classified as risky. NordVPN found more than 10,000 fake fashion stores. The two studies measure different things, so the numbers do not add up.

Is the one in eleven figure from this year?

No. The one in eleven figure for risky Black Friday domains is from Check Point's 2025 research. The 2026 figure we found is almost one in five, though it is a different measure.

Can an AI assistant send me to a fake shop?

NordVPN says Perplexity recommended two fraudulent stores and that ChatGPT and Gemini cited sites connected to the fake network. Treat shop links from chatbots like any search result and check the address yourself.

What is the safest way to pay?

A credit card, or your phone's wallet, because they allow disputes and keep the real number from the shop. Avoid gift cards, bank transfers and crypto.

How do I report a fake shop?

Tell your card issuer first. Then report it to your national consumer authority. In the US that is ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and the FBI's IC3 also takes complaints.
