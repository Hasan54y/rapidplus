Android November 2026 Update: Wallet Fills Your CVV, Quick Share Previews and Easier Storage Cleanup

No new Android version needed. Every change in Google's November system updates, in plain words, and how to make sure your phone has them.

Rapid+ Team · 26 Nov 2026 · 6 min read

On this page

Written on 26 November 2026. This is based on Google's own System Services release notes and on 9to5Google's report of 23 November. The two lists differ in a few places, and we say where. Google rolls these updates out gradually, so a feature listed here may take a few weeks to reach your phone.

Your Android phone gets a quiet update from Google every month, even when your phone maker sends nothing. The November 2026 round brings a few things you will notice: Google Wallet can now fill in your card's security code for you, Quick Share shows a preview of a YouTube link before you accept it, a new storage tool helps you clear large files, and your photos can back up to a Windows PC over Quick Share. Here is everything in plain words, and how to make sure you have it.

The short version

  • Wallet fills in your CVV. Google Play services v26.46 (23 November) lets Google Wallet autofill a saved card's security code on card checks, after you confirm it with your screen lock.
  • Quick Share previews YouTube links. The person receiving a shared YouTube link sees a preview before accepting it.
  • Easier storage cleanup. v26.45 (16 November) shows cleanup progress and lets you swipe through large files to keep or delete them.
  • Photos to Windows. v26.43 (2 November) adds automatic backup of photos and videos from Android to a Windows PC through Quick Share.
  • Play Store age controls. Play Store v54.2 lets you manage age range sharing per app in Play settings.
  • Play Protect reminder. Play Store v53.9 reminds you if Play Protect has been turned off for a while.

What Google System Updates are

Android updates come from two places. Your phone maker sends the big Android version updates and the monthly security patch. Google, separately, updates a set of apps and services that sit underneath Android on almost every phone with the Play Store: Google Play services, the Play Store itself, Android WebView (which shows web pages inside apps), and the Google Play system update. Google publishes a list of these changes every month, tagged by device type, such as phone, Wear OS watch, TV, car or PC.

That is why a two-year-old phone can still get new Wallet or Quick Share features without a full system update.

Google Wallet: security codes filled in for you

The most practical change in November is for online shopping. Google's notes say Play services v26.46 adds autofill of saved card security codes (CVV or CVC) on card verification screens in Google Wallet, after you authenticate with your screen lock. In other words, when a checkout asks you to confirm your card, you can confirm with your fingerprint, face or PIN instead of finding the card.

Two other Wallet items arrived earlier in the month in v26.44 (9 November): a Traveler QR feature, and support for residential door keys that work with the Matter Aliro standard, through Google Home. Google's notes do not say which countries get these first.

A word of caution for this week: an autofilled code is still only as safe as the shop you are on. With thousands of fake Black Friday sites online, check the address before you let your phone fill in anything. Our Black Friday fake domains report shows what to look for.

Quick Share: YouTube previews and backup to Windows

Two Quick Share changes landed this month:

  • Link previews. In v26.46, when someone sends you a YouTube link over Quick Share, you see a preview before you accept the transfer. That makes it easier to refuse something you did not expect.
  • Backup to Windows. In v26.43, Google lists automatic backup of photos and videos from Android to Windows through Quick Share. Google's note is short and does not give setup steps, so look for the option in Quick Share on your PC and phone over the coming weeks.

Storage cleanup and Family Hub

Play services v26.45 (16 November) brings storage management updates under your Google account: you can track your cleanup progress, review large files by swiping to keep or delete them, and see which Google account is being managed. If your phone keeps warning you that storage is full, our step by step guide on freeing up space on Android and iPhone walks you through every option.

Earlier, v26.43 added access to the Android Family Hub from Android Settings, which gathers family controls in one place. Play Store v54.2 also adds app-level controls for sharing your age range with apps, managed in Play settings, and Play services v26.44 gives developers a way to ask for the age range of the person using the device. These come as more countries and US states require age checks, a topic we covered in age verification and privacy.

Security and Play Store changes

  • Play Protect reminder (Play Store v53.9, 2 November): if Play Protect, Google's app scanner, stays switched off for a while, the Play Store reminds you. Keep it on, especially if you install apps from outside the Play Store.
  • Password Manager imports (v26.43): when you import passwords and passkeys that clash with ones already saved, Google Password Manager now lets you choose which to keep.
  • WebView v158 and v159 (9 and 18 November): security and privacy improvements and bug fixes for the component that shows web pages inside apps.
  • Play Store extras: a large watch face preview on app pages (v54.1), Play Achievements for gaming milestones (v54.2), and quicker sign-in state updates for Play Games (v54.0).

Two small differences between the sources: 9to5Google lists an outdoor workout feature where a watch uses the phone's GPS to save battery, and an on-screen gamepad on the Pixel Fold, which we did not find on Google's own page. Google's page also lists an Android AICore update dated 25 November for faster AI model loading on phones and PCs, which came after 9to5Google's article.

How to make sure you have the update

  1. Update Google Play services. Open Settings, Apps, then find Google Play services and open its app details in the Play Store. If an Update button shows, tap it.
  2. Update the Play Store. In the Play Store, tap your profile picture, Settings, About, then Update Play Store.
  3. Install the Google Play system update. Its date is shown under Settings, About phone, Android version, or under Security and privacy on many phones. Tap it to check for an update and restart if asked.
  4. Update apps. In the Play Store, open Manage apps and device and update everything, which includes Android System WebView.
  5. Be patient. Google rolls out server-side features over several weeks, so a feature can be missing even when your version number matches.

Menu names vary between phone makers, so if you cannot find an option, search for it in the Settings app.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a new Android version to get these features?

No. These come through Google Play services and the Play Store, which update on most Android phones regardless of the Android version, as long as the phone is supported.

Is it safe to let Google Wallet fill in my CVV?

Google requires your screen lock before it fills in the code. The bigger risk is the website itself, so only use it on shops you know, reached by typing the address or using their app.

Why don't I see the new features yet?

Google releases them gradually. Update Play services and the Play Store, then wait a few weeks. Some features are also limited to certain countries or devices.

What is Android WebView?

It is the part of Android that shows web pages inside apps. It gets security fixes almost every month, so keep it updated in the Play Store.

Where does Google list these changes?

On its Google System Services release notes page, linked below, updated through each month.
