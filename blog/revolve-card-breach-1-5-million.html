Revolve Card Breach: What Was Taken, How Many People Are Affected and What to Do

Customers who saved a card lost names, card numbers and expiry dates. What is confirmed, what is not, and the five-minute steps to protect yourself.

Rapid+ Team · 24 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

On this page

Written on 24 November 2026. This relies on Malwarebytes' report of 3 November, which draws on Revolve's SEC filing and state attorney general listings. We read Malwarebytes' translated version and did not read the filing itself. Revolve has not confirmed a total number of people affected, and we mark which numbers are estimates.

Revolve, the online fashion retailer, says someone took payment card details from customer accounts. The company has not said how many people were hit, but figures found in state filings point to as many as 1.5 million. The people at risk are customers who had saved a card in their Revolve account. If you have shopped there, the steps below take about five minutes.

The short version

  • Revolve Group Inc. disclosed that an unauthorised party obtained customer data, including names, contact details, card numbers and expiry dates.
  • Only customers who had saved a payment card to their Revolve account were affected.
  • The count is unconfirmed. Texas lists 127,200 Texas residents. Malwarebytes found a figure of 1,515,073 in data behind the Texas listing, which is not on the public list and which Revolve has not confirmed.
  • Texas records give 11 September as the date of the incident and 29 September as the date it was found, though the year is not shown in the text we read.
  • Revolve's filing says the data was likely obtained by "automated means". It has not said how.

What happened

Revolve filed a notice with the US Securities and Exchange Commission on 29 October, as Malwarebytes reads it. The filing says an unauthorised party likely obtained customer data by automated means, which can mean software that tries large numbers of logins or requests. It gives no total, and no group has claimed the attack. Revolve says it took additional security measures but does not say which.

Malwarebytes also cites a customer notice sent to the Massachusetts Attorney General. It does not say when letters went to customers.

What was exposed

DataStatus
Names and contact detailsExposed
Card numbersExposed. It is unclear whether they were shown in full or masked.
Card expiry datesExposed
CVV security codeNot mentioned in the filing, according to Malwarebytes

Do not read "no CVV" as "safe". Malwarebytes warns that a card number and expiry date can be enough for some online purchases even without the security code, so the card still needs attention.

It is also unclear whether the card data sat on Revolve's own systems or with a payment processor. Malwarebytes says that is unknown.

How many people

This is the part to be careful with, because several sites repeat a number that is not official.

  • 127,200: the Texas residents listed on the Texas attorney general's site.
  • 1,515,073: a total Malwarebytes found in data behind that site. It is not on the public list.
  • Other sites repeat the larger number but appear to rely on the same Texas data.
  • Revolve has not confirmed any total, and its filing does not give one.

The honest answer is that it could be well over a million, and that nobody outside the company can say for sure.

What to do if you shop at Revolve

  1. Check your statements for small charges you do not recognise. Thieves often test a card with a tiny purchase first.
  2. Report anything odd to your card issuer at once, using the number on the back of your card.
  3. Ask for a new card if you had one saved. Issuers can cancel and reissue quickly, often within days.
  4. Remove saved cards from shopping accounts. A password manager or your phone's wallet is safer than a card stored at every store.
  5. Be cautious about messages about the breach. Scammers use breach news to send fake notices. Do not click links, and call the number on your card to check.
  6. Change your Revolve password, and any account where you used the same one.

Why saved cards are a risk

Saving a card is convenient, and in this breach it is exactly what put customers at risk. The more places that hold your number, the more places that can leak it. Your phone's wallet, such as Apple Pay or Google Pay, gives stores a one-time token instead of your real number, so a breach at the store does not expose your card. Our guide to paying safely online explains why a credit card also gives you the strongest dispute rights.

Why the timing matters

This breach came to light just before Black Friday, when millions of people enter card details online. It is a good moment to tidy what you have saved. Our look at this year's fake shopping domains covers the other risk of the season.

Frequently asked questions

Was my Revolve account hacked?

Only if you had a card saved there, and even then the company has not said how many accounts were touched. If you never saved a card, the filing does not suggest your payment data was taken.

How many people are affected?

Revolve has not said. Texas lists 127,200 residents, and Malwarebytes found a figure of about 1.5 million in data behind the Texas listing. The larger number is unconfirmed.

Was my CVV stolen?

The filing does not mention CVV numbers, according to Malwarebytes. Still, card number plus expiry can be enough for some purchases.

How did the attackers get in?

Revolve has not explained. Its filing says the data was likely obtained by automated means. It does not name a method or a group.

Should I cancel my card?

If you had a card saved at Revolve, asking your issuer for a new one is a sensible precaution. It is quick and free with most banks.
