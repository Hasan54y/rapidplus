Phone Storage Full? How to Free Up Space on Android and iPhone Without Losing Anything

Most phones hold gigabytes you can clear in minutes. The safe, official ways to get space back on Android and iPhone, quickest wins first.

Updated 26 November 2026. Steps are taken from Google's and Apple's own help pages. Menu names differ between phone makers and software versions, so if a step does not match your phone, search for the setting by name in the Settings app.

"Storage almost full" is one of the most common phone warnings, and it usually appears at the worst moment, just as you try to take a photo or install an update. The good news is that most phones hold gigabytes you can clear in a few minutes without losing anything that matters. This guide covers Android and iPhone, starting with the quickest wins and the safe way to do each one.

The short version

  • Find out what is using space first: Settings, Storage on Android, or Settings, General, iPhone Storage on iPhone.
  • Photos and videos are usually the biggest item. Back them up, then remove the copies on the phone.
  • Empty the trash. Deleted photos stay in Recently Deleted or Trash for 30 days and still take up space.
  • Archive or offload apps you do not use. Both keep your data and remove the app itself.
  • Clear junk files with Files by Google on Android.
  • Old message attachments add up. On iPhone you can make Messages delete old conversations automatically.

Step one: see what is taking up space

Android

Open the Settings app and tap Storage, then tap a category to see what is inside. On Android 14 and newer, Google's help page also describes a Free up space option under Storage, which lists photos, videos and apps you have not used recently.

iPhone

Open Settings, General, then iPhone Storage. You will see recommendations at the top and a list of apps sorted by size. Tap Show All to see every recommendation, then Enable on the ones you want. Apple notes that some space is used by cached files, which the iPhone removes by itself when it needs room.

Photos and videos: the biggest win

Back up before you delete anything. Once your photos are safely in the cloud, you can remove the copies stored on the phone.

Google Photos on Android

  1. Make sure backup is on and finished.
  2. In Google Photos, tap your profile picture or initial, then Free up space on this device.
  3. Google shows how much space you will get back. Tap Free up to delete the backed-up copies from your phone.

Google says photos and videos fewer than 30 days old may stay on your device, but they are still in your Google Photos library. Be careful with one thing: deleting a photo inside the Google Photos app deletes it from your device and your backup too. Use Free up space, not delete, if you want to keep it online.

Android can also do this automatically. Under Settings, Storage, Free up space, open the menu, Settings, and turn on Smart Storage, which removes backed-up photos and videos when storage is almost full.

iCloud Photos on iPhone

Turn on iCloud Photos under Settings, your name, iCloud, Photos, and make sure Optimize iPhone Storage is on. Apple keeps the full-quality originals in iCloud and smaller copies on the phone. Apple gives every account 5 GB of free iCloud storage, which fills quickly, so you may need more space or a computer backup. iOS 17 and newer also offer a Recommended for You view to review large files, and you can merge duplicate photos.

Empty the trash, or nothing changes

This is the step most people miss. On iPhone, deleted photos go to Recently Deleted and stay there for 30 days. To clear them now, open Photos, Collections, Recently Deleted under Utilities, tap Select, choose items and delete them.

In Google Photos, deleted items stay in Trash for 30 days. To empty it, open Collections, Trash, then the menu and Empty Trash. On Android, Storage cleanup moves files to the Trash as well, so check it after you clean.

Apps you rarely use

Archive apps on Android

On Android 15 and newer, you can archive an app: open Settings, Apps, choose the app, and tap Archive. Google says archiving removes the app's software, permissions and temporary files and stops notifications, but keeps the icon and your data. Tap the faded icon later to reinstall it. Or uninstall apps you never use.

Offload apps on iPhone

In Settings, General, iPhone Storage, tap an app and choose Offload App, which frees the space used by the app but keeps its documents and data. To have this happen automatically, go to Settings, Apps, App Store and turn on Offload Unused Apps. The icon stays with a small cloud symbol, and tapping it reinstalls the app.

Clear junk files and app caches

On Android, open Files by Google, tap Clean, and on the Junk files card tap the Clean button, then Clear. Google says this removes temporary files and does not change app settings, but the deletion is permanent. Files by Google can also find duplicate files, old screenshots and large files.

You can also clear one app's cache in Settings, Apps, choose the app, then Storage. Know the difference: Clear cache deletes temporary data, and the app may be slower the next time you open it. Clear storage deletes all of the app's data, including sign-ins and settings, so avoid it unless you mean to reset the app.

Downloads, messages and other hidden space

  • Offline video and music. Remove downloads you have finished with in YouTube, YouTube Music, Netflix, Spotify or the Apple TV app.
  • Old messages on iPhone. Go to Settings, Apps, Messages, Keep Messages, and choose a shorter period than Forever. Apple warns that conversations and their attachments older than that period are then removed automatically, so save any photos you want first.
  • WhatsApp and chat apps. Most have their own storage manager in settings that lists large videos and forwarded files.
  • The Downloads folder. PDFs, installers and saved images build up quietly. Delete what you do not need, or copy it to a computer first.
  • Your browser. A browser's cache and offline pages can grow large too. In Rapid+ you can clear browsing data per profile.

Google's November 2026 system update also brings a new storage cleanup tool that lets you swipe through large files to keep or delete them, as we explain in our November update guide.

What not to do

  • Do not install "cleaner" or "booster" apps from unknown developers. Many show ads or ask for broad permissions, and your phone already has the tools above.
  • Do not delete before you back up. Check that your backup has finished.
  • Do not tap Clear storage on an app unless you are ready to set it up again.
  • Do not ignore the warning for long. A nearly full phone can fail to install security updates.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my storage still full after deleting photos?

Deleted photos stay in Recently Deleted on iPhone or Trash in Google Photos for 30 days. Empty it to get the space back.

Is clearing the cache safe?

Yes. Clearing an app's cache removes temporary data only, although the app may load more slowly the next time. Clear storage is different and removes all of the app's data.

What is the difference between archiving and uninstalling an app?

Archiving on Android, or offloading on iPhone, removes the app but keeps your data and the icon, so reinstalling picks up where you left off. Uninstalling removes both.

Will Google Photos Free up space delete my photos?

No. It removes only the copies on your phone that are already backed up. They stay in your Google Photos library.

Do phone cleaner apps help?

Usually not. The built-in tools and Files by Google do the same job without ads or extra permissions.
