Firefox's Free VPN Has No Data Cap Until 31 January: What It Covers and What It Does Not

The cap is gone for a few months, but the VPN protects only traffic inside Firefox. Here is what that means in practice and where it falls short.

Rapid+ Team · 18 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

On this page

Written on 18 November 2026. The facts come from Help Net Security and OMG! Ubuntu, which reported Mozilla's change. We did not find a statement on logging or data handling in that reporting, so we do not make claims about it. Read Mozilla's own privacy notice before you rely on the service.

Since 10 November 2026, Firefox's free built-in VPN has had no monthly data cap. Mozilla lifted the 50 GB limit until 31 January 2027, and on 1 February the cap returns. The offer is useful, and it is also easy to misunderstand, because this VPN protects only the traffic that goes through Firefox.

This article explains what changed, what a browser-only VPN covers and what it leaves outside, and when it makes sense to switch it on.

The short version

  • The free VPN launched with Firefox 149 in March 2026 with a 50 GB monthly limit. From 10 November 2026 to 31 January 2027 there is no cap.
  • You need a free Mozilla account to turn it on.
  • It covers traffic inside Firefox and nothing else on the device. Mail clients, chat apps and games use your normal connection.
  • It is available on desktop and Android in supported regions. At launch these were the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany and France. iOS support is planned without a date.
  • It works in private windows, and you can set per-site exceptions so chosen sites bypass the VPN.
  • Mozilla also sells Mozilla VPN, a separate paid product that covers the whole device.

What changed

When the feature launched, free users had 50 GB of VPN traffic each month. That is plenty for browsing, and not enough for hours of video. From 10 November the limit is gone for a set period. Mozilla ran a similar promotion earlier this year that raised the number of server locations to 28 for a time. Offers of this kind are marketing decisions, so plan on the 50 GB limit coming back on 1 February 2027.

What a browser-only VPN covers

A system VPN creates an encrypted tunnel for everything your device sends. A browser VPN creates one only for the browser's own requests. The difference shows up in everyday use.

TrafficGoes through Firefox's VPN?
Web pages loaded in FirefoxYes
Private windows in FirefoxYes
Sites you set as exceptionsNo, they use your normal connection
Email apps, chat apps, gamesNo
Other browsers on the same deviceNo
System updates and background servicesNo

What the VPN does is replace your visible IP address with the VPN server's address for the sites you visit in Firefox, and hide the contents of your connection from the network you are on. That helps on public Wi-Fi and with simple location-based tracking. For a longer comparison of what each tool hides, see our article on proxies and VPNs.

What it does not hide

  • Your accounts. If you sign in to a site, the site knows who you are, whatever address you use.
  • Your browser fingerprint. Screen size, fonts and other traits still identify a browser. Our article on fingerprinting explains why a VPN does not change that.
  • Cookies. A site that set a cookie earlier can still recognise you.
  • What the VPN provider sees. A VPN moves trust from your network to the provider. The reporting did not describe Mozilla's logging, so check its policy.

A VPN is one layer. It is not a switch that makes you anonymous.

When it is worth switching on

  1. On public Wi-Fi. At a cafe, hotel or airport, the VPN keeps the other people on the network from seeing the sites you visit.
  2. When your network blocks or throttles certain sites. A VPN can help, though it does not guarantee access, and some services block known VPN addresses.
  3. For a quick region test. Developers and shoppers sometimes check how a page looks from another country.

It is less useful for banking, where your bank may flag a login from an unusual place, and for streaming services that block VPN addresses.

How to try it

  1. Update Firefox to a current version. The free VPN arrived in version 149.
  2. Sign in with a Mozilla account. Creating one is free.
  3. Turn on the VPN from the Firefox interface. The reports we read do not give the exact menu steps, so look for the VPN option after signing in.
  4. Add exceptions for sites that misbehave behind a VPN, such as your bank.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Firefox VPN really free?

Yes, with a Mozilla account and within the limits. Normally it has a 50 GB monthly cap, which is lifted from 10 November 2026 to 31 January 2027. The cap returns on 1 February 2027.

Does it protect my whole phone or computer?

No. It protects traffic inside Firefox only. Mozilla's separate paid product, Mozilla VPN, covers the system.

Does it work in private browsing?

Yes. According to OMG! Ubuntu, it also works in private mode, and you can set exceptions for individual sites.

Does a VPN make me anonymous?

No. It changes the address that sites see and hides your traffic from the local network. Logins, cookies and your browser fingerprint can still identify you.

Which countries can use it?

It is offered in supported regions. At launch these were the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany and France. Check Firefox for the current list.
