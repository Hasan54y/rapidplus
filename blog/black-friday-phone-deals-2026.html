Black Friday Phone Deals 2026: How to Spot a Real Deal and Avoid Phone Scams

Which? found 92 percent of tracked deals were no cheaper than other times. A checklist for buying a phone this Black Friday without regret.

Rapid+ Team · 26 Nov 2026 · 6 min read

On this page

Written on 26 November 2026. The buying advice comes from the consumer group Which? (articles of 19 November 2026 and 20 November 2025) and the scam warnings from Cleveland19's report of 12 November 2026. Which? is a UK group, so its prices are in pounds, but the checks work in any country. We could not read a ConsumerAffairs article on the same topic, so it is not used.

A new phone is one of the most popular Black Friday buys, and one of the easiest to get wrong. Some "deals" are the normal price with a crossed-out number above it. Some are phones meant for another country. And some are not shops at all. Black Friday falls on 27 November this year, so here is how to tell a real phone deal from a bad one, and how to spot the scams that copy big retailers.

The short version

  • Check the price history, not the "was" price. Which? found that 92 percent of Black Friday deals it tracked were available at the same price or cheaper at other times of the year.
  • Last year's model is usually the best value. Which? says waiting about 12 months after launch can save hundreds.
  • Count the years of updates. Google promises seven years of support on phones since the Pixel 8, Apple five years, and Xiaomi, according to Which?, two years.
  • Avoid grey imports and unknown marketplace sellers. The phone may not work fully on your network or have a local warranty.
  • Never trust a link in a text about a delayed order, and never call a customer service number from a sponsored search result.

Is it really a deal?

Which? advises using a price tracker before you buy, such as Keepa or CamelCamelCamel for Amazon, or PriceSpy for other shops. Its rule is simple: if the deal price is not a true historical low, it is not a real deal. The same article cites the group's research finding that 92 percent of Black Friday deals were available for the same price or less at other times of the year.

Look at where the discount comes from too. Which? notes that Samsung often discounts its own phones directly, while Apple rarely offers cash discounts on its own site, so real iPhone discounts tend to come from large retailers.

Which phone gives you the most for your money

Buy last year's model

Which?'s November 2026 guide says brands regularly discount older models and that waiting about a year after launch can save you hundreds. Its advice is to shop for a phone released in the previous year.

Do not overpay for premium

The same guide says phones costing £200 to £350 in its tests had the best battery performance, and suggests aiming for a mid-range phone with a top budget of about £500. Mid-range lines such as the Pixel "a" or Galaxy "A" series are often the best Black Friday value, paired with a SIM-only plan.

Check how long it gets updates

Security updates matter as much as the camera. A phone that stops getting fixes becomes riskier every month. Which? lists Google at seven years from launch on phones since the Pixel 8, Apple at five years, and Xiaomi at two. A cheaper phone that loses support next year is not a bargain. Divide the price by the years of support left to compare fairly.

Contracts, trade-ins and hidden costs

  • Contracts. Which? says you will often pay more overall on a contract than buying the phone outright, calling many contracts a high-interest loan in disguise. It also warns about mid-contract price rises. If you can, pay for the handset upfront and pick a cheap SIM-only plan.
  • Trade-ins. Get an independent valuation before accepting a retailer's "enhanced" trade-in. Values vary widely between shops, and Which? notes you usually have about 14 days to send the old phone after the new one arrives.
  • The charger. Apple and Samsung no longer include a plug in the box. Which? says an official fast charger can cost £20 to £40 and warns that cheap unbranded chargers can be a fire risk.
  • Refurbished phones. Which? suggests buying refurbished only with at least a 12-month warranty, from a well-reviewed seller.

Before you trade in or sell your old phone, back it up, sign out of your accounts and reset it. That removes your photos, messages and passwords for good.

Grey imports and marketplace sellers

A grey import is a genuine phone bought for another country and sold in yours. Which? warns these may have no valid local warranty and may not support all of your country's 4G and 5G bands, which means weaker signal or missing features. On marketplaces, check the seller's detailed ratings, and on Amazon check whether the item is sold by Amazon or by a third party. A price far below every major shop is a warning, not a win.

The phone scams that peak on Black Friday

Cleveland19's report quotes a ConsumerAffairs writer on the tricks that appear every year:

  • Ghost websites. Fake shops promoted through social media ads, with prices that seem too good to be true. Before you click, search the company name with the word "scam".
  • Fake closing-down sales. Ads for "store closing" or "final close-out" sales that lead to fake sites built to collect your details.
  • Fake customer service numbers. Scammers buy sponsored search listings for big shops' support lines, which can appear above the real number. Use the shop's app or official website to find the number.
  • Delivery problem texts. A message saying your order has a problem, with a link to fix it. A real company will not text you to verify a tracking number or address.

The scale is real this year. Check Point counted more than 4,000 new Black Friday themed web addresses, almost one in five of them risky, as we reported in our Black Friday fake domains article. Our older guide to spotting fake shops has a full checklist.

Your phone deal checklist

  1. Type the shop's address yourself or use its official app.
  2. Check the price history in a tracker.
  3. Prefer last year's model or a mid-range phone.
  4. Check how many years of updates are left.
  5. Confirm the phone is meant for your country and has a local warranty.
  6. Add up the total cost of any contract.
  7. Pay by credit card or your phone's wallet, never by gift card or bank transfer.
  8. Know the return policy and keep the receipt.

Shopping on your phone? A separate browser profile for shopping keeps a rushed click away from your email and bank.

Frequently asked questions

Are Black Friday phone deals worth it?

Some are, many are not. Which? found 92 percent of the deals it tracked were available at the same price or cheaper at other times, so check price history first.

Should I buy a phone on contract on Black Friday?

Which? says buying outright with a SIM-only plan often costs less overall. Add up the full contract cost and check for mid-contract price rises.

What is a grey import phone?

A genuine phone made for another country. It may lack a local warranty and may not work on all your network's 4G and 5G bands.

How do I know a phone shop is real?

Type the address yourself, search the name with the word "scam", check reviews and the payment options, and be wary of prices far below every big retailer.

Is a refurbished phone safe to buy?

It can be. Which? suggests choosing one with at least a 12-month warranty from a reputable refurbisher.
