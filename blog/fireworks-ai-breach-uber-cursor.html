Fireworks AI Breach: How One Supplier Exposed Uber Drivers and Cursor Developers

Stolen keys at an AI supplier led to leaked driver data and a poisoned code extension. What happened and how drivers and developers should respond.

Rapid+ Team · 23 Nov 2026 · 6 min read

On this page

Written on 23 November 2026. This is based on BleepingComputer's reports on Uber and Cursor (14 and 17 November), which cite Cursor's incident post and a filing with the Maine Attorney General. We did not read those primary documents. Uber has not named Fireworks AI itself, so that link rests on TechCrunch's reporting.

A breach at an AI infrastructure company called Fireworks AI is spreading to the companies that use it. Uber has told 149,346 drivers that their driver's licence numbers and other details were taken. Cursor, the AI code editor, says attackers used a stolen key to publish a malicious copy of a popular coding extension, which about 5,000 developers may have installed. Notion has also said that AI chat transcripts were exposed. This article puts the pieces in order.

The short version

  • Fireworks AI, which provides AI model hosting for other companies, disclosed several breaches in October 2026, including exposed keys and a compromised cloud provider.
  • Uber says a service provider told it on 9 October that an unauthorised party had compromised the provider, and on 13 October Uber concluded the party used credentials to access Uber data.
  • Uber notified 149,346 drivers, according to a filing in Maine. Exposed: names, driver's licence numbers, dates of birth and addresses.
  • Cursor says attackers used a key stored at Fireworks to copy data from a storage bucket, then used credentials found in it to reach more systems.
  • On 29 October, malicious copies of Anthropic's Claude Code extension were placed in the cache that serves Cursor's extension marketplace. They stayed live for about 11 hours.

What Fireworks AI is

Fireworks AI runs infrastructure for training and running AI models. Companies send it data and requests, so it holds keys and storage that belong to its customers. When a company like that is breached, the harm lands on every customer at once.

According to BleepingComputer, Fireworks disclosed several incidents in October 2026. One involved exposed API keys and tokens. Another involved attackers reaching developer machines through a compromised third-party cloud provider. BleepingComputer says Fireworks attributed an attack to a financially motivated group linked to "LLMjacking", the theft of access to AI models.

Uber: 149,346 drivers

Uber's account is that a service provider notified it on 9 October that an unauthorised third party had compromised that provider. On 13 October Uber determined the party had used credentials to access Uber data. Letters went to affected drivers on 10 November.

A filing with the Maine Attorney General puts the number at 149,346 and lists names, driver's licence numbers, dates of birth and addresses. Uber did not name Fireworks. The link comes from TechCrunch, as reported by BleepingComputer, and Uber's spokesperson did not respond to BleepingComputer in time. One date is unclear: BleepingComputer says the breach came in early October but also cites TechCrunch for an incident at Fireworks on 24 October.

Cursor: a poisoned extension

Cursor is an AI code editor built on the same base as Visual Studio Code. Its incident post, as summarised by BleepingComputer, says attackers took an access key stored at Fireworks and used it to copy data from a read-only storage bucket that Cursor shared with Fireworks. The bucket held records of AI coding sessions, mostly from 2025, from users who did not have Privacy Mode on. Some contained plain-text API credentials, which gave access to further Cursor systems.

Cursor's figures: fewer than 0.05 percent of users had session records exposed, and fewer than 0.01 percent of customers had API credentials compromised. Data copied from its internal tools included archived non-production source code, limited customer support emails, product usage data and credentials some customers had shared with support.

The malicious extension

On 29 October the attackers placed malicious copies of Anthropic's Claude Code extension, versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313, on the cache that serves Cursor's extension marketplace. They were live for about 11 hours before removal. The malware tried to install a persistent SSH backdoor. Researchers found coding errors that likely stopped it from working on many Windows and Mac machines where SSH was not already on. Anthropic's own extension and the Open VSX store were not compromised.

Cursor estimates up to 5,000 users who requested the affected versions in that window may have received the bad builds. That is an estimate, not a confirmed count. Cursor says production systems, official app releases, customer code repositories and the code and prompts of Privacy Mode users were not affected.

Notion and others

BleepingComputer also reports that Notion said its AI chat transcripts were exposed through the Fireworks breach. We did not read Notion's own notice, and the full list of affected companies is not known.

What developers should do

  1. Check your editor. If you use Cursor, follow its remediation guidance, which it links from its incident post. Look at which version of the Claude Code extension you installed and when.
  2. Look for SSH changes. Check for unexpected keys in your SSH authorised keys file and for new background services.
  3. Rotate keys that touched AI tools. Anything pasted into an AI chat or coding session may be in a log somewhere.
  4. Use Privacy Mode or equivalent where a tool offers it, and keep secrets out of prompts.
  5. Ask your vendors where your data goes. Your AI tool may send it to other companies you never chose.

What affected drivers should do

  • Watch for fake Uber messages. Scammers use leaked data to sound real. Open the Uber app yourself instead of following links.
  • A driver's licence number can support identity fraud. Consider a credit freeze or fraud alert where you live, and read your breach letter for any monitoring offer.
  • Be wary of calls that quote your address and date of birth, and use only the official Uber support route.

The larger lesson

None of these companies was breached first. One supplier was, and the damage followed the keys. The same pattern showed up in the Optimizely attack and in the vendor breaches of the same week.

Frequently asked questions

Was Uber itself hacked?

Uber says a service provider was compromised and an unauthorised party used credentials to reach Uber data. It did not name Fireworks, but TechCrunch reported the link.

How many people are affected?

Uber notified 149,346 drivers, per a Maine filing. Cursor says fewer than 0.05 percent of its users had session records exposed and estimates up to 5,000 developers may have received the bad extension.

What was the malicious extension?

Two fake builds of the Claude Code extension, versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313, served from Cursor's marketplace cache for about 11 hours on 29 October. Anthropic's real extension was not compromised.

Did the malware work?

It tried to install an SSH backdoor. Researchers found coding errors that likely stopped it on many Windows and Mac systems without SSH already on, but some systems may have been exposed.

Should I stop using AI coding tools?

Not necessarily, but treat them as suppliers: check where your data goes, use privacy settings, and keep secrets out of prompts.
