Android Developer Verification: What Changes for Sideloaded Apps

Apps must come from a verified developer, even outside the Play Store. The timeline, the advanced sideloading flow and what stays the same.

Rapid+ Team · 18 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

On this page

The short versionThe timelineWho is affectedWhy Google says it is doing thisWhat to doFAQSources
Written on 18 November 2026. The timeline comes from Help Net Security (31 March 2026) and Android Authority, which reported Google's plan. Sources differ slightly on some dates and on how the sideloading steps work, and we mark those points. Check Google's Android developer documentation for the definitive rules.

Google is changing how apps get onto Android phones. Starting with four countries on 30 September 2026, apps installed on certified devices must come from a developer whose identity Google has verified, even if the app is installed from outside the Play Store. Installing unverified apps will still be possible, but with extra steps. Most people will notice nothing at first, and the change reaches every region in 2027.

This article sets out the timeline, what changes for someone who installs apps from outside the store, and what stays the same.

The short version

  • Google calls the rule "developer verification". It checks who published an app, not what the app does. Google says it confirms developer identity and does not review app content.
  • From 30 September 2026, in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand, apps must be registered to a verified developer to be installed or updated on certified Android devices.
  • The requirement is scheduled to reach all regions in 2027.
  • Apps from unverified developers can still be installed through an "advanced flow" or with ADB, the developer tool. Reports describe a restart and a 24-hour wait in the advanced flow.
  • Students and hobbyists can use a limited distribution account to share apps with up to 20 devices, with no government ID and no fee, according to Android Authority.

The timeline

WhenWhat
April 2026The Android Developer Verifier, a system service that checks whether an app is registered to a verified developer, is introduced.
June 2026Early access to limited distribution accounts for students and hobbyists.
August 2026Limited distribution accounts and the advanced sideloading flow launch globally, per Help Net Security.
30 September 2026Registration is required in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand for installs and updates on certified devices.
Rest of 2026Rollout stays limited while Google gathers feedback.

Android Authority lists the first stores where the checks apply: Google Play, Samsung's Galaxy Store, Xiaomi GetApps, HONOR App Market, OPPO App Market, vivo V-Appstore and Palm Store. Google says millions of apps have been registered since developer verification opened in March.

Who is affected

Everyday users

If you install apps from Google Play or your phone maker's store, your experience stays the same. The developers of those apps will have registered, and Google says that for most users the installation experience does not change.

People who install apps from other sources

Installing an APK downloaded from a website is called sideloading. If the app is registered to a verified developer, it should install as usual. If it is not, Android shows extra steps. According to Help Net Security and Android Authority, unverified apps can be installed with ADB or through the advanced flow, which Android Authority says includes a mandatory 24-hour lock and several installation steps. The reports we read do not describe the lock in detail, and one summary does not describe ADB as an exemption, so check the current developer documentation.

Developers

Developers register through the Android Developer Console for apps distributed outside Google Play, or through the Play Console. Hobbyists and students can use the limited distribution account for small audiences. Developers who share apps widely outside the store will need to complete verification.

Why Google says it is doing this

Malicious apps often arrive through sideloading: a fake banking app sent by text, a modified game, a "free" version of a paid tool. Stores can remove such apps once they are noticed. Developer verification aims to make it harder for a bad actor to publish under a disposable identity. The identity check does not stop malware by itself, and Google has said it does not review content, so the benefit is accountability and traceability.

Critics of such rules usually argue that they give one company control over what runs on a phone and burden independent developers. The reporting we read describes Google's plan and its safeguards, such as the advanced flow and the hobbyist account, and does not provide a full critique, so we leave that debate to the sources you trust.

What to do

  1. Prefer official stores for apps. They add a layer of review and verification and make updates automatic.
  2. Check the publisher before sideloading. A website you trust, a developer with a track record and a matching signature are better signs than a link in a message.
  3. Expect extra steps if you sideload apps from small developers once the rule reaches your region. Keep a list of the apps you installed that way.
  4. Keep your phone updated. The verification system is part of Android's services, and security updates stay important.
  5. Be wary of anyone telling you to bypass the steps. A message that urges you to disable protections to install an app is a classic scam.

Frequently asked questions

Is sideloading being banned?

No. Reports say Google is not removing it. Apps from unverified developers can still be installed with extra steps, either through ADB or an advanced flow.

When does it affect my country?

On 30 September 2026 the rule began in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand. Google plans to expand it globally in 2027.

Does Google review the apps?

Google says verification confirms developer identity and does not review app content.

What about hobby developers?

A limited distribution account lets students and hobbyists share an app with up to 20 devices without a government ID or a fee, according to Android Authority.

Does it apply to every Android phone?

It applies to certified Android devices. Reports mention Android 7 and newer in the initial markets. Check Google's documentation for your device type.
