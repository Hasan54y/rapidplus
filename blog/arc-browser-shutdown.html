Arc Is Shutting Down on 22 December: What to Do With Your Browser Data

The Browser Company has set a hard end date for Arc. Here are the deadlines, what carries over to Dia, and a checklist for picking your next browser.

Rapid+ Team · 18 Nov 2026 · 6 min read

On this page

The short versionThe timelineWhy the company says it is stoppingWhat carries over to DiaHow to choose your next browserWhat to do before 22 DecemberFAQSources
Written on 18 November 2026. The dates and details below come from The Browser Company's announcement as reported by TechCrunch and 9to5Mac on 22 October 2026. We did not find the company's step-by-step export instructions in the reporting, so check Arc's own help pages for those.

The Browser Company has announced that Arc, the browser with the vertical sidebar and "Spaces", will stop working on Mac and Windows on 22 December 2026. New accounts closed the day of the announcement. If you use Arc every day, you have about five weeks left to decide where your tabs, passwords and bookmarks will live.

This article lists the dates, what the company says will carry over to its newer browser Dia, and how to judge any browser you might move to. The last part matters more than the brand name, because Arc's end shows what happens when a browser's maker decides it can no longer afford to keep it safe.

The short version

  • The Browser Company announced the end of Arc on 22 October 2026. New Arc accounts can no longer be created.
  • On 23 November, Arc Search on iOS and Android loses its AI features, and sign-in and tab sync stop. It keeps working as a basic local browser.
  • On 22 December, Arc stops working entirely on Mac and Windows, accounts are deactivated and Arc Search is removed from Google Play.
  • The company says personal data is deleted within 30 days after accounts are deactivated. A report on its help article gives 15 December as the last day to ask for a copy of your data or for deletion.
  • The company's stated reason is security: "Keeping a browser secure takes constant, dedicated work that is only increasing in the age of AI."

The timeline

Within 30 days after thatPersonal data is deleted, according to TechCrunch.

Arc's development had already stopped well before this. Reports in May 2025 said the company had moved its focus to Dia, an AI-centred browser, and Atlassian bought The Browser Company in 2025 for $610 million, according to TechCrunch. The October announcement turns that quiet stop into a hard end date.

Why the company says it is stopping

The company's explanation is about the cost of safety. A browser is a large program that opens untrusted code from the whole internet all day. It needs frequent security updates, and the work does not end. In our article on the Chrome zero-day patched on 9 November, one bug in one update was enough to prompt an emergency release. A small team maintaining two browsers has to pick one.

The company also told users that Dia "was built from the ground up to meet today's security standards" and that its engineers will focus there. That is the company's claim about its own product, and we have not tested it. The plain reading is simpler: Arc will not receive security fixes after the shutdown, and a browser without fixes becomes unsafe to use.

What carries over to Dia

The company says that passwords, bookmarks, extensions and history can move to Dia, and that Spaces, folders and tab groups import with their data. It describes the mapping like this:

  • Arc's Spaces become Dia "Profiles", used to separate work from personal browsing.
  • Folders become "Pinned Tab Groups".
  • Vertical tabs remain as an optional sidebar layout.
  • Arc's browser features stay free in Dia, and the AI features are optional.

The reporting we read does not say what does not carry over, and it does not describe an export route for people who want to leave for a different browser. If you do not want Dia, export your bookmarks and passwords from Arc before 22 December, and check Arc's help pages for how to do it.

How to choose your next browser

Instead of asking which browser is the newest, ask questions that Arc's ending has made concrete.

  1. Who pays for the security work? A browser backed by a large company, a foundation or a paying user base has a better chance of staying patched than a side project of a team that is changing direction.
  2. How quickly does it ship fixes? Look at the release notes. Chromium-based browsers should publish a new version within days of a Chrome security release.
  3. Can you leave? A good browser lets you export bookmarks, passwords and history in standard formats. Arc's ending is a reminder to test that before you need it.
  4. What does it send out? Check the settings for tracking protection, telemetry and account sign-in. An account is not required to browse.
  5. Does it support what you rely on? Profiles, vertical tabs and extensions are the things people most often miss after leaving Arc.

Rapid+ Browser is one option, and it also offers separate profiles, but we are not the right judge of your needs. Whatever you choose, read how it handles updates before you move your logins into it.

What to do before 22 December

  1. Decide whether you want Dia or another browser.
  2. If you are moving to Dia, use its import feature from Arc while Arc still works.
  3. If you are leaving, export bookmarks and passwords now, and keep the file somewhere safe. A passwords file is not encrypted, so delete it after the import.
  4. Request a copy of your data or deletion before 15 December if you want either.
  5. Remove Arc from your computer after the move, so an unpatched browser is not left installed.

Frequently asked questions

Will Arc stop working immediately?

No. Arc stops working on Mac and Windows on 22 December 2026. Until then it runs as before, but it receives no new features, and signing up for a new account is no longer possible.

What happens to Arc on my phone?

Arc Search on iOS and Android loses its AI features on 23 November, and sign-in and tab sync stop. It keeps working as a basic local browser, and it is removed from Google Play on 22 December.

Can Arc be made open source?

Users have asked for it. CEO Josh Miller said earlier that open-sourcing Arc would also mean opening the Arc Development Kit, which powers Dia and is still core to the company's value, so there is no plan to do it at the moment.

Is it safe to keep using Arc after 22 December?

It will not run on Mac or Windows after that date, according to the announcement. Any copy that keeps working would not receive security fixes, so you should not rely on it.

Do I have to use Dia?

No. Dia is the company's recommended path and it offers an import, but any browser that can import bookmarks will do. Export your data first.
