Outage Week: Apple, Chase, United, the NSA and the FAA All Failed, and Why It Was Not a Cyberattack

Five outages, five stated causes. The week air traffic, banking, iCloud and a classified network stumbled, and what officials said.

Rapid+ Team · 24 Nov 2026 · 6 min read

On this page

Written on 24 November 2026. This draws on 9to5Mac, CNBC, NBC News and Nextgov. A Reuters report quoting a US official on cyberattacks could not be read, so we rely on what each company and agency said itself. Every cause below is the organisation's own statement, not an independent finding.

In one week in mid-November 2026, Chase's banking apps, Apple's iCloud and App Store, United Airlines, the NSA's classified network and US air traffic control all had trouble. The timing made people suspect a coordinated attack. Each organisation said otherwise: software updates, a bug and a router. This article lays out what each one said, what is still unclear, and why a run of outages can look like an attack when it is not.

The short version

  • 13 November: a nationwide US ground stop halted flights for more than four hours. The Transportation Secretary blamed a router in Arkansas.
  • 16 November: a United Airlines systems outage delayed departures, and Chase online, mobile and branch transactions failed for hours.
  • 17 November: Apple iCloud, App Store and other services went down. Apple blamed an internal update.
  • 17 and 18 November: the NSA's classified network, NSANet, was degraded. The NSA said it was scheduled maintenance.
  • United, Apple, Chase and the NSA each said or were reported to say that it was not a cyberattack.

13 November: the FAA ground stop

CNBC reports that a nationwide ground stop stopped departures for more than four hours. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed a router in Arkansas that overwhelmed the systems feeding radar and other air traffic control data. FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford called the upgrades routine maintenance to move from copper wires to fibre optic cable. CNBC and Nextgov also link other pieces suggesting the root cause was still unclear, so the explanation is not fully settled.

16 November: United Airlines

United had what CNBC calls a widespread system outage that briefly delayed departing flights. The pilots' union described multiple pilot-facing applications being affected. United said it had resolved a technology issue and that there was no indication it was a cyberattack, and that it was not related to the ground stop of the Friday before. Flights in progress and United Express flights were not affected.

16 November: Chase

NBC News reports that Chase online and mobile transactions failed along with in-person branch transactions. Customers got error messages and had trouble withdrawing money, and Zelle payments were delayed. Chase said it had a temporary issue related to a software bug in an on-premise hardware system, and that it was not cyber-related. By about 6:30 p.m. Eastern the bank said transactions were posting normally. NBC notes Chase has about 94 million customer relationships. How many accounts were hit was unclear. Zelle said it was not down. PNC and Capital One reported no direct effect.

17 November: Apple services

9to5Mac says iCloud, the App Store, Apple Pay, Apple Support, iPhone activation, Apple Music and App Store Connect for developers were affected, and that some people were signed out of iCloud or saw verification errors. Apple's status page still showed services as available while outage reports piled up. Apple said an internal update caused some outages that morning, and ruled out a cyberattack, according to a post by journalist Mark Gurman quoted in the article. Consumer services came back first, while developer services still had issues at the last update.

17 and 18 November: NSANet

Nextgov reports, citing six people familiar, that NSANet, the classified network the NSA uses to move top-secret data, slowed analysts' work and forced staff to change arrival times. Employees got a Code Orange message that closed the campus to most staff on Tuesday night, then a Code Gray notice setting staggered arrivals. The NSA said it experienced temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance and that mission-critical capabilities remained fully operational. Four sources said there were no immediate signs of a cyberattack, and that it may be linked to a series of software upgrades. A former NSA technology official said intelligence collection was not interrupted.

Were they connected?

NBC says there is no evidence these incidents are connected. Each organisation gave a different cause, and none of the explanations links to another. That does not prove nothing is shared. It means that, on the public record, they look like separate failures that happened in the same week, along with the Microsoft 365 outage of 12 and 13 November.

Why failures cluster and look like attacks

  • Big systems change constantly. Updates, migrations and upgrades are routine, and every change is a chance for a bug. Microsoft's outage came from a change made months earlier.
  • A few providers carry a lot. When a payment, cloud or network system fails, thousands of services fail with it.
  • Outages and attacks look the same from outside. Both show up as error pages and silence.
  • Denials are not proof. A company's statement that it was not an attack is a claim, not an audit, though several companies gave specific causes.

A real attack did happen the same week at another US agency, in the USDA payments breach, which shows why it is worth checking each case separately.

How to cope with outages

  1. Keep a second payment method. Carry another card or keep cash for a day.
  2. Have a backup for travel. Download boarding passes and note airline phone numbers.
  3. Know where to check. Use official status pages and the company's social posts, not rumours.
  4. Watch for scams. Fake "outage compensation" or "account restore" messages appear within hours. Go to the company's site yourself.
  5. Do not redo big transactions in the middle of an outage. Wait for confirmation to avoid double charges.

Frequently asked questions

Were the outages caused by a cyberattack?

United, Apple, Chase and the NSA each said or were reported to say it was not. Officials blamed a router for the ground stop. These are the organisations' own explanations.

Were the outages related?

NBC says there is no evidence they are connected. The stated causes are different for each.

What caused the Apple outage?

Apple said an internal update caused some outages, according to a post quoted by 9to5Mac. It was not a cyberattack.

Did I lose money in the Chase outage?

Chase said transactions were posting normally by early evening. If something looks wrong on your account, contact Chase and keep records of what happened.

Was the NSA network hacked?

The NSA said it had temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance. Sources told Nextgov there were no immediate signs of a cyberattack.
